An AI agent for a Daybook vault

It writes in pencil.
You decide what goes in ink.

The agent reads your week, asks, and drafts the brief, the posting and the close. Every line it writes links its source, which pkm checks before you see it. It never rewrites your words, deletes, or sends anything in your name.

Set it up → How it works

For people who already keep, or want to keep, a Daybook. The method works without an agent.

Who does what

You write and decide.
pkm finds and copies.
The agent reads, asks and drafts.

You

in ink

  • write entries and marks
  • decide what to post, close and send

The words are yours, and what to decide about your own work and life is a judgment only you can make.

pkm

stamped, exact

  • finds lines by name, link and words
  • copies entries word for word
  • checks every source and link

Both must be exact, and a tool does the same thing every time.

The agent

in pencil

  • reads only the lines pkm found
  • notices what matters and raises issues
  • asks, proposes and drafts, then stops

It can go over a week of entries and notice a promise made in passing, or a name that keeps coming back.

Posting, the way it is done

It proposes. You answer by number. pkm copies.

The agent never types your entries into a note, so they cannot change on the way. pkm refuses to post an entry twice, so asking again does no harm.

On request, never on its own

What you can ask it for.

Brief
What is new, important and needs you, from what you wrote since the last brief.
Posting
A numbered proposal of what goes where; you answer by number; pkm copies it word for word.
Close
It asks about each open task and question, and records what you decide.
Check-in
At most five questions about whether your notes are still true, answered in a word.
Rounds
What changed for you on the boards and code your projects link to, or everything new on one of them, with tasks to catch up. Read only.
Recall
Your own questions, asked back on a schedule. The study pack proposes new ones from a course.
Find
An answer from your notes with its sources, or "not found".
Preparation
A draft agenda for a 1:1, or an update on a project, every point sourced. Drafts only.

Why you can trust it

It doesn't assume the model is right.
It makes its work checkable.

Each way a language model is known to fail has a guard, and most of the guards are pkm, not the model.

The nine rules
FailureGuard
An invented fact, date or quoteEvery claim needs a source, pkm evidence --verify checks it exists, and "unknown" is allowed
Your words drifting as they are postedpkm copies each entry character for character
Too much posted, or the wrong thingA numbered proposal; nothing is posted without your yes
A label that hides the factsIssues as facts with sources, never a health colour or a score
An instruction hidden in an email or a ticketText is data; the searching agent can only read
Private notes reaching the modelFolders you name as off limits are never read

Cheap, and honest about it

It reads little.
It can still be wrong.

Why it stays cheap

  • It never reads the vault to answer: one-line summaries first, then the few lines pkm finds.
  • A fixed reading budget. When it runs out, it says what it didn't search, and asks.
  • pkm is a command line, not a server of tools: a command named in a skill costs one line.

What it does not establish

  • No study has measured whether an agent makes a note system better kept or more used.
  • The brief can miss something that matters and stress something that doesn't. It is a reading, not a verdict.
  • An agent costs money, and you send its provider what it reads.

so The method works without one. Add the agent when the reading it saves you is worth more than that.
